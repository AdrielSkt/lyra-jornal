Headline

Grammys 2027 reopen the question: what counts as dance music?

Fred again.., Skrillex and Robyn are among the dance/electronic nominees. John Summit responded by saying he will no longer submit his music.

The Recording Academy announced its 2027 Grammy nominees on Monday (Nov 16). Best Dance/Electronic Recording includes Fred again.. with Jamie T (“Lights Burn Dimmer”), Madonna, Tame Impala and three first-timers: Slayyyter, underscores and Tinashe. Best Album nominees are Madonna, Robyn, Skrillex (Soma), Slayyyter and Tame Impala. The ceremony is on February 7 in Los Angeles.

The same day, John Summit posted on X that he would stop submitting his tracks, arguing that pop names dominate a category that should reflect what clubs actually play. LYRA isn't taking sides, but notes the data point: none of the Recording nominees appear in the Beatport club-genre top 10s in this snapshot.

For producers, the week brought another angle on the same debate: a track with an AI vocal reached No. 1 in Beatport's techno chart (see the news). What counts as “electronic music”, and who sings in it, is up for discussion.

— RA · Billboard BR · Billboard BR (John Summit)

Tech column

The UK shuffle has taken over house

The Deep House top 10 now averages ~129 BPM, House and Tech House sit at ~130 and UK Garage at ~134. CHRIS STASSY charts in House, Minimal and Deep House; Interplanetary Criminal in House, Minimal and UK Garage.

DOOn a 4x4 groove at 132, keep the kick straight and apply swing only to hats and percussion: in Ableton, drag a 16th-note swing groove (Swing folder of the Grooves library) onto the hat clips and set Amount to ~70%. Add a ghost clap on the 4th 16th of beat 2.

WHYA straight kick holds the floor; the garage bounce lives in the high elements. Swing on everything makes the groove sloppy — swing on top only gives that bouncing feel without losing drive.

REF.“Dangerous” (CHRIS STASSY, Interplanetary Criminal, 135) in the House top 10 and “On 2nite” (Silva Bumpa, 136), UK Garage #1.

Track X-ray

“On 2nite” — Silva Bumpa (136 BPM, Ab minor)

Context: UK Garage / Bassline #1 on Beatport, on Room Two Recordings, a label that also lands “PEOPLE IN THE PLACE” in the Minimal top 3.

What the style asks for: a straight 4x4 kick, heavily swung hats and shakers, claps on 2 and 4 with ghost notes before them, and a short, bouncing bass that answers the kick instead of rolling over it.

To recreate the idea: start with just kick and bass at 136, let the bass sit on the 16ths between kicks, and only then add swing to the highs. Arrange in 8-bar blocks, with hard cuts instead of long builds.

— Beatport

Anatomy of a sound

The rolling tech house bassline, stock devices only

Sound: Operator or Drift with a saw wave, low-pass filter around 400 Hz and a short envelope (decay ~150 ms). A touch of Saturator for harmonics that survive phone speakers.

Rhythm: 16ths with root and octave, removing the notes that land on the kick. 55–58% swing.

Fit: a short kick sidechain (Compressor, fast attack, ~80 ms release) and Utility in mono below 120 Hz.

Myth or fact

“Deep house is always slow”

Myth, at least on the charts. The Deep House top 10 on Beatport averages ~129 BPM in this snapshot, and the #1 sits at 130. The label now describes tone (round lows, jazzy chords, space) more than tempo. The 118–122 classics still exist; they just aren't at the top.

— Beatport

From the archive

Model 500 — “No UFO's” (1985)

In tribute to Juan Atkins. Released on his own Metroplex label, it is a landmark of Detroit techno: a dry drum machine, a repetitive synth bass and a sci-fi mood.

What it taught: that you can make dance music with machines and very little, as long as the groove and the sound have identity. Nearly all the techno in today's charts descends from that idea.

— RA

Cut out & take to the studio · challenge of the week

Swing on top only

DOBuild 4 bars at 132: a straight 4x4 kick with no swing, and hats + shaker with a 16th swing groove from the Groove Pool. Make two versions — swing on everything vs. swing on the highs only — and compare.

WHYIt's the quickest way to hear where the garage bounce lives: a firm kick holds the floor, swing on the highs gives the bounce.
