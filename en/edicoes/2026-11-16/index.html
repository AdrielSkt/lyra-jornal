Ableton announces Live 13

A new step sequencer, updated Racks, ARA 2 and a multi-engine Drum Synth. Public beta is already open.

Ableton announced Live 13, with the final release expected in early 2027. The headline features are a step sequencer, reworked Racks, ARA 2 support (direct integration with audio editors) and a Drum Synth with several synthesis engines.

According to Mixdown, anyone who buys a Live 12 license before launch gets the upgrade. unconfirmed Current Live 12 users should check their own upgrade path on Ableton's site.

The step sequencer and Drum Synth are aimed squarely at drum programming. The same week, Resident Advisor published a survey of 1,099 artists' gear guides: Live is the most cited DAW.

Tech column

House sped up

The average BPM of the Tech House and House top 10s is now ~130. Melodic H&T holds at ~126; Afro and Organic at ~123.

DOTake a 126 BPM house groove and try it at 130. Keep the swing but dial it back: from ~60% to ~55% in Ableton's groove pool.

WHYHigher BPM means more energy, and heavy swing at high tempo starts to stumble. The floor is pulling toward minimal/deep tech and UK sounds, already at 132–135.

REF.NO STATIC “F.D.A.U.” and Sapian “Takes Me” (130); Beave “Zoolander” (132).

Track X-ray

“Zoolander” — Beave (132 BPM, E minor)

Core idea: very few elements, lots of space. The groove comes from the dialogue between a dry kick, a short off-kick bass and percussion that drops in and out.

To recreate: a short, deep kick, a one-note bass in shifted 16ths, an open hat on the offbeat and a single stab every 8 bars. Change by subtraction, not addition.

Typical structure for the style: 32-bar drums-only intro, bass enters, short kickless break, back in with the stab.

— Beatport

Anatomy of a sound

The rolling tech house bassline, stock devices only

Sound: Operator or Drift with a saw wave, low-pass filter around 400 Hz and a short envelope (decay ~150 ms). A touch of Saturator for harmonics that survive phone speakers.

Rhythm: 16ths with root and octave, removing the notes that land on the kick. 55–58% swing.

Fit: a short kick sidechain (Compressor, fast attack, ~80 ms release) and Utility in mono below 120 Hz.

Myth or fact

“You need paid plugins to sound pro”

Myth. Much of this week's top 10 comes down to a well-chosen kick, a simple bass and a spacious arrangement. Ableton's stock devices (EQ Eight, Compressor, Saturator, Drift) cover the essentials; what separates amateur from pro is sound selection, gain staging and arrangement.

Word of the week

Swing

Slightly delaying the even notes of a subdivision (e.g. the 2nd and 4th 16th) so the groove “sways” instead of sounding like a metronome. Think of a deliberate, rhythmic limp.

Cut out & take to the studio · challenge of the week

Rolling bassline at 130

DOOn a tech house groove at 130, write a 1-bar bass using only root and octave, in 16ths, avoiding the kick hits. Any synth works: a saw with the filter around 400 Hz.

WHYThe “roll” comes from the bass filling the gaps between kicks. Moving notes off the kick avoids low-end clashes before you even sidechain.

REF.The basslines of “F.D.A.U.” (NO STATIC) and “Takes Me” (Sapian).
