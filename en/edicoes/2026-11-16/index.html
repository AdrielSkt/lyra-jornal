Headline

A track with an AI singer tops Beatport's techno chart

Charlotte de Witte's “Azadi”, featuring the virtual voice NAVA, is No. 1 in Peak Time Techno. The EP's proceeds go to women's rights.

The title track of Charlotte de Witte's Azadi EP (Persian for “freedom”), out on her own KNTXT label, sits at the top of Beatport's Techno (Peak Time / Driving) Top 100 this Monday. It is one of the few top-10 tracks not on Drumcode, which holds 6 of the 10 spots.

The voice belongs to NAVA, a virtual singer created with AI in 2025 by British-Iranian digital artist Farbod Mehr. According to Resident Advisor, the project stands for Iranian women who cannot sing in public, and all proceeds from the EP go to Femena, which supports women's rights defenders in the Middle East and North Africa.

Why it matters to producers: an AI-generated vocal at No. 1 in a major Beatport genre opens a practical debate about credits, rights and what audiences accept as a “voice”. LYRA just records the fact and the sources; the debate belongs to the scene.

— Beatport · RA

Tech column

Peak-time techno went major

In the Peak Time Techno top 10, 9 of 10 tracks are tagged in a major key. House, Organic House and UK Garage are the opposite: 9 of 10 in minor. (Keys as listed by Beatport; automatic detection can be wrong.)

DOWrite a 1-bar riff in B-flat major using only root, fifth and major third. Duplicate it and swap the major third for a minor third (D → D-flat). In Ableton Live 12, turn on the clip's scale mode so you stay in key. Compare both over the same groove at 132–135.

WHYThe third is the note that decides whether a chord sounds “bright” (major) or “dark” (minor). In peak-time techno, a major third brings euphoria without a long melody — which is why it shows up so often in remixes of classics and one-note riffs.

REF.“We Come 1” (Adam Beyer Remix, B-flat major, 134) and “Space Date” (HNTR & KASIA Remix, B-flat major, 132), both on Drumcode.

Track X-ray

“On 2nite” — Silva Bumpa (136 BPM, A-flat minor)

On paper: UK Garage / Bassline No. 1, Room Two label, 136 BPM, A-flat minor. The same artist and label sit in the Minimal / Deep Tech top 3 with “PEOPLE IN THE PLACE” (133).

What the style asks for: firm kick and clap, heavily swung 16th hats, a short, bouncy bass talking to the kick, and a chopped vocal as the hook. Fewer layers, more rhythm.

Typical structure for the style: 16–32-bar drum intro, bass comes in, short break, back with the vocal. Listen via the link and check where each part lands.

— Beatport

Anatomy of a sound

UK garage swing, stock devices only

Beat: in a Drum Rack, kick on beat 1 and the “and” of 3 (the broken “2-step” pattern), or on all four beats for the 4x4 version; clap on 2 and 4.

Swing: 16th hats with a swing groove from the Groove Pool in the 60–66% range. Vary hat velocity (loud, soft, medium, soft) so they “talk”.

Bass: Operator or Drift with a sine or square wave, short notes off the kick, light Saturator and a short kick sidechain on Compressor.

From the archive

Model 500 — “No UFO's” (1985)

In tribute to Juan Atkins. Released on his own Metroplex label, it is a landmark of Detroit techno: a dry drum machine, a repetitive synth bass and a sci-fi mood.

What it taught: that you can make dance music with machines and very little, as long as the groove and the sound have identity. Nearly all the techno in today's charts descends from that idea.

— RA

Cut out & take to the studio · challenge of the week

Same riff, two moods

DOOver a techno groove at 134, write a 1-bar riff in B-flat minor. Duplicate it and change only the third (D-flat → D) to make it B-flat major. Post both versions, 8 bars each.

WHYIt trains your ear to hear what a single note does to emotion — the same principle separating this week's peak-time techno (major) from its house (minor).

REF.“We Come 1” (Adam Beyer Remix) in major; “Dangerous” (CHRIS STASSY, Interplanetary Criminal) in C minor.
